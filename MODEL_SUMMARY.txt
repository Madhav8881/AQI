DELHI AIR POLLUTION FORECASTER - PROJECT SUMMARY
================================================
(Written so that anyone can follow it, even without knowing the project.)


1. WHAT IS THIS PROJECT?
------------------------
Delhi has some of the most polluted air in the world. This project is a
computer program (a deep-learning model) that FORECASTS how polluted the air
will be at 36 places in Delhi over the next 24 hours.

  - Input : the last 24 hours of pollution readings from the 36 stations.
  - Output: the expected pollution at each station 1, 3, 6, 12 and 24 hours
            from now.
  - Also  : a web app where anyone can pick a station, see the forecast on a
            map, read health advice, and check how right past forecasts were.

What is "PM2.5"?
  PM2.5 = tiny dust and smoke particles in the air, smaller than 2.5
  micrometres (about 30 times thinner than a human hair). They go deep into
  the lungs, so a higher number means dirtier, more harmful air.
  It is measured in "ug/m3" (micrograms per cubic metre of air).
  Example: 25 = clean, 100 = unhealthy for many people, 300+ = severe.

What is "AQI level"?
  India groups PM2.5 into six colour-coded levels:
  Good, Satisfactory, Moderate, Poor, Very Poor, Severe.


2. HOW DOES IT WORK? (in simple steps)
--------------------------------------
  Step 1  Collect: years of real hourly readings from 36 monitoring stations.
  Step 2  Clean: remove broken values, fill small gaps (using only past data).
  Step 3  Teach: show the model the past, one 24-hour window at a time, and let
          it learn "when the last day looked like this, the next hours looked
          like that".
  Step 4  Test: check it on a period it has NEVER seen (the last 2 years).
  Step 5  Use: the app feeds it today's live readings and shows the forecast.

The main model is called a GAT-Transformer. In plain words:
  - Transformer part = learns the pattern over time (morning rush, night
    build-up, rising or falling trend).
  - GAT part = lets each station "listen" to its nearby stations, because
    pollution drifts from one area of the city to another.
We compare it with simpler models (see section 7) to prove it is better.


3. THE DATA
-----------
  What : real hourly PM2.5 readings from 36 Delhi air-quality stations
         (government networks CPCB, DPCC and IMD).
  When : January 2023 to September 2026 (32,856 hours per station).
  Where from:
    - XKDR Air Quality API (Jan 2023 - Aug 2025)
        file: data/raw/delhi_pm25_2023_2025_raw.csv
    - Kaggle dataset "Delhi Air Quality CPCB Hourly 2010-2026"
        (yug201/delhi-air-quality-cpcb-hourly-2010-2026), used for
        Jan 2025 - Sep 2026
        file: data/raw/delhi_pm25_cpcb_2025_2026.csv
      The two sources matched exactly on 117,055 readings they share.
  Quality: 93.5% of the values are real measurements. The rest were filled
           in using only earlier readings (never future ones).
  Live data (for the "Prediction vs reality" page): readings from the World
         Air Quality Index website (aqicn.org), collected hourly.


4. WHAT THE MODEL USES TO FORECAST (the "factors")
--------------------------------------------------
For each station it looks at the last 24 hours. For every hour it uses:
  1. The PM2.5 reading.
  2. Whether that reading was really measured or filled in.
  3. Time of day   - captures traffic and night-time patterns.
  4. Day of week   - weekday vs weekend activity.
  5. Time of year  - captures seasons (winter smog, monsoon cleaning).
It also uses:
  - Which station it is (each station has its own typical level).
  - The 8 nearest stations, weighted by distance (the "graph").
  - Whether pollution has been rising or falling over the last day.

NOT used:
  - Weather (temperature, humidity, pressure, wind). We tested it: it
    improved forecasts by only 2-3%, so it was left out to keep things simple.
  - Wind direction and other pollutants (left out by choice).
  - Fires, traffic counts, festivals, rain forecasts.
Because of this, forecasts far ahead (12-24 hours) are less accurate, and
sudden events (Diwali fireworks, a change of wind) can be missed.


5. HOW IT WAS TRAINED AND TESTED
--------------------------------
The data is split by TIME, like real use (train on the past, predict the
future):

  Training    Jan 2023 - Jun 2024    12,905 samples  (the model learns here)
  Validation  Jul 2024 - Sep 2024     2,184 samples  (used to pick the best
                                                       settings)
  TEST        Oct 2024 - Sep 2026    17,393 samples  (~2 years, never seen,
                                                       used only to score it)

  - Each model was trained 3 times with different random starts, and the
    results are averaged, so the score is not a lucky one.
  - For the live app, the models were trained again on ALL data up to
    Sep 2026, so they know the latest conditions. Nothing is re-trained
    day by day.


6. HOW GOOD IS IT?  (scored on the 2 unseen test years)
-------------------------------------------------------
Quick summary in words:
  - It explains about 75% of how Delhi's pollution goes up and down.
  - On average its forecast is off by about 27 ug/m3, which is 25% better
    than the basic forecast (36 ug/m3).
  - 1 hour ahead it names the correct AQI level 74% of the time, and is
    right or only one level off 98.5% of the time.
  - Overall (1 to 24 hours ahead) it is right or only one level off 94% of
    the time.

Details (all forecast times together):

                                      Our model         Basic forecast*
                                      (GAT-Transformer)
  How much of the changes it explains   75%               57%
  Average error                         27 ug/m3          36 ug/m3
  Correct AQI level                     58%               51%
  Correct AQI level or one level off    94%               87%

  * Basic forecast = simply repeats the current reading ("tomorrow will be
    like now"). It needs no learning. Any useful model must beat it.

By how far ahead:

  Hours ahead   Correct level   Right or 1 off   Average error   Explained
       1             74%            98.5%          14 ug/m3         93%
       3             61%            95.6%          24 ug/m3         82%
       6             54%            93.2%          29 ug/m3         72%
      12             51%            91.3%          33 ug/m3         66%
      24             49%            90.4%          35 ug/m3         62%

  The further ahead, the harder it is - this is normal for pollution.

Only the most recent period (2026): our model's error is 22.0 ug/m3 vs 29.1
for the basic forecast.


7. WHY WE USED A GAT (GRAPH ATTENTION NETWORK)
----------------------------------------------
The idea: air pollution is not local. Smoke and dust drift across the city, so
what is happening at a station's neighbours now often shows up at that station
a few hours later. LSTM and Transformer models look at ONE station at a time
and cannot use this. The graph model connects each station to its 8 nearest
neighbours and LEARNS how much to trust each one ("attention"), starting from
a distance-based guess (closer = more trust).

Models compared (error in ug/m3, lower is better):
  Basic forecast 36.1 | LSTM 27.7 | Transformer 27.4 | GAT-Transformer 26.9

Evidence from our own tests:

  1. It is the most accurate model almost everywhere.
     Best at 35 of the 36 stations (average error 26.8 ug/m3, against 27.2
     for the Transformer and 27.5 for the LSTM).

  2. The gain is real, not luck.
     A statistical test (Diebold-Mariano) shows it beats the LSTM at 1, 3, 6
     and 12 hours ahead, and the Transformer at 1, 3 and 6 hours ahead
     (p < 0.001). At 24 hours all models are about equal.

  3. It keeps working when a sensor breaks (the biggest advantage).
     Sensors fail in real life. We hid each station's own readings and asked
     the models to forecast it from its neighbours only:
        Robust LSTM (no graph)       error rises by 48%   (27.6 -> 40.9)
        Robust GAT-Transformer       error rises by 17%   (26.8 -> 31.4)
     Both were trained the same way; the only difference is the graph. The
     graph model fills the gap from nearby stations; the LSTM cannot.
     ("Robust" = trained with random stations switched off. Without this, all
     models fail badly when a sensor breaks, about +90%. That is why the live
     app uses the Robust GAT-Transformer.)

  4. It is useful in practice. In the app you can pretend a station's sensor
     is broken and it still forecasts that station, and shows which
     neighbours it relied on.

  Honest note: on normal days the graph's gain over the plain Transformer is
  modest (26.9 vs 27.4 ug/m3, about 2%). Its main value is in short and
  medium-range forecasts and in sensor failure. We did not run a test with the
  graph links removed, so the gain is shown by comparison with the other
  models, not by switching the graph off.


8. HOW TO READ THE NUMBERS (ug/m3 vs %)
---------------------------------------
  ug/m3 = the UNIT of pollution (micrograms of particles per cubic metre).
    "An error of 27 ug/m3" means the forecast was 27 units away from reality
    (e.g. predicted 180, real 153).
  %     = a share or comparison, NOT a unit of pollution.
    "Correct AQI level 58%" = 58 out of 100 forecasts were in the right level.
    "25% better" = our error is 25% smaller than the basic forecast's error.
  Error as a %: the average pollution in the test period was about 99 ug/m3,
    so an average error of 27 ug/m3 is about 27% of the usual level (about
    15% for forecasts 1 hour ahead).
  "Explained" (R2) = how much of the ups and downs of pollution the model
    captures. 100% = perfect, 0% = no better than guessing the average.
  "Correct level" is strict: predicting 59 when the truth is 61 counts as
    wrong if the two fall in different levels. "Right or 1 off" is lenient.


9. THE APP (what a user sees)
-----------------------------
  Today                    live pollution at every station on a map, health
                           advice, and the forecast for the next 24 hours.
  Prediction vs reality    what the model predicted compared with what the
                           sensors later measured.
  How good is it?          the test results and model comparison above.
  About                    a plain-language explanation.

  To run it:  python -m streamlit run app/streamlit_app.py
  (full install and training instructions are in README.md)


10. LIMITATIONS (honest notes)
------------------------------
  - Accuracy drops for forecasts further ahead (normal for air pollution).
  - Without weather information, sudden changes can be missed.
  - Live readings are converted from AQI values to ug/m3, so they can differ
    slightly from the official CPCB files.
  - Two stations (ITO, Shadipur) are not available in the live feed.
  - The live comparison covers only the days the collector was running.
