DELHI PM2.5 FORECASTING - MODEL SUMMARY
=======================================

WHAT THE MODEL DOES
  Predicts PM2.5 (a measure of air pollution) at 36 Delhi stations for the
  next 1, 3, 6, 12 and 24 hours, using the last 24 hours of readings.
  Model: GAT-Transformer (a graph network that lets nearby stations share
  information, plus a Transformer that learns the time pattern).
  Final forecast in the app = average of GAT-Transformer, Robust
  GAT-Transformer and Transformer ("Ensemble").


FACTORS USED BY THE FORECASTER (live app and test)
  For each station the model looks at the last 24 hours. Every hour has
  8 inputs, all derived from the PM2.5 readings and the clock:
    1. PM2.5 reading (scaled per station)
    2. Was it really measured? (yes/no flag, so filled gaps are known)
    3-4. Time of day (hour, so daily traffic/night patterns are learned)
    5-6. Day of week (weekday vs weekend)
    7-8. Time of year (seasons: winter smog, monsoon cleaning)
  Also used:
    - Which station it is (each station has its own learned profile)
    - The 8 nearest stations, weighted by distance (the graph / GAT part),
      so pollution moving across the city is picked up
    - The shape of the last 24 hours (rising or falling)
  NOT used:
    - Weather (temperature, humidity, pressure, wind). Tested with
      Open-Meteo data: only 2-3% better, so left out.
    - Wind direction and other pollutants (excluded by choice)
    - Fires, traffic counts, festivals, rain forecasts
  Because of this, forecasts 12-24 hours ahead are less accurate, and
  sudden events (e.g. Diwali fireworks, a wind change) can be missed.


DATASET
  Real hourly PM2.5 readings from 36 Delhi air-quality monitoring stations
  (CPCB / DPCC / IMD).

  Source 1: XKDR air quality API (Jan 2023 - Aug 2025)
            file: data/raw/delhi_pm25_2023_2025_raw.csv
  Source 2: "Delhi Air Quality CPCB Hourly 2010-2026" on Kaggle
            (yug201/delhi-air-quality-cpcb-hourly-2010-2026),
            used for Jan 2025 - Sep 2026
            file: data/raw/delhi_pm25_cpcb_2025_2026.csv
            (matched the XKDR data exactly on 117,055 overlapping readings)

  Total: 32,856 hours (Jan 2023 - Sep 2026) x 36 stations.
  93.5% of the values are real measurements; the rest were filled in
  using only past data. No wind direction or other pollutants are used.

  Live data (for the "Prediction vs reality" page): World Air Quality
  Index (aqicn.org) feed, collected hourly.


TRAINING AND TEST DATA  (test run used to measure how good the model is)
  Training   Jan 2023 - Jun 2024     12,905 forecast samples
  Validation Jul 2024 - Sep 2024      2,184 forecast samples (picks best model)
  TEST       Oct 2024 - Sep 2026     17,393 forecast samples (~2 years)

  The test data was never used for training or for any choice.
  It is the future relative to the training data, as in real use.
  Each model was trained 3 times (3 random seeds); results are the average.

  FINAL MODELS used in the live app were retrained on all data up to
  Sep 2026 (about 32,500 samples) so they know the latest conditions.


HOW TO READ THE NUMBERS (ug/m3 vs %)
  ug/m3 (micrograms per cubic metre) = the UNIT of PM2.5: how many
    micrograms of tiny particles are in one cubic metre of air.
    Example: "PM2.5 is 180 ug/m3". An error of 27 ug/m3 means the forecast
    was 27 units away from the real reading (e.g. predicted 180, real 153).
  % = a share or comparison, NOT a unit of pollution.
    "Correct AQI level 58%" = 58 out of 100 forecasts were in the right level.
    "25% better" = our error is 25% smaller than the basic forecast's error.
  Error as a %: average PM2.5 in the test period was about 99 ug/m3, so an
    average error of 27 ug/m3 is about 27% of the usual pollution level
    (about 15% for forecasts 1 hour ahead).
  R2 score = how much of the ups and downs of pollution the model explains
    (100% = perfect).


HOW WELL THE MODEL DOES ON THE 2-YEAR TEST DATA
  Averaged over all forecast times (1-24 hours ahead):

                                     GAT-Transformer    Basic forecast*
    Explains PM2.5 changes (R2)      75%                57%
    Average error                    27 ug/m3           36 ug/m3
    Correct AQI level                58%                51%
    AQI level within +-1             94%                87%

  * Basic forecast = just repeats the current reading (no learning).

  By how far ahead:

    Hours ahead   R2     Correct level   Within +-1 level   Avg error
         1       0.93       74%            98.5%           14 ug/m3
         3       0.82       61%            95.6%           24 ug/m3
         6       0.72       54%            93.2%           29 ug/m3
        12       0.66       51%            91.3%           33 ug/m3
        24       0.62       49%            90.4%           35 ug/m3

  Other models, average error over all hours (lower is better):
    Basic forecast 36.1 | LSTM 27.7 | Transformer 27.4 |
    GAT-Transformer 26.9 | Robust GAT-Transformer 27.0

  Only 2026 data: GAT-Transformer has an error of 22.0 ug/m3 vs 29.1 for the
  basic forecast.

  If a station's sensor stops working, the Robust GAT-Transformer's error
  rises only 17% (Robust LSTM: 48%) because it borrows from neighbours.

  "Levels" are the Indian air-quality bands: Good, Satisfactory, Moderate,
  Poor, Very Poor, Severe.


HONEST NOTES
  - Accuracy drops for forecasts further ahead (normal for air pollution).
  - Correct-level accuracy is a strict measure; "within +-1 level" is lenient.
  - The graph (neighbouring stations) gives a modest gain over the plain
    Transformer (26.9 vs 27.4 ug/m3).
  - Live readings are converted from AQI to ug/m3, so they can differ a
    little from CPCB's own files.
